      My hand turkey

      Find a scrap of paper and something to draw with. A pencil, pen or crayon will do.

      1. Spread one hand on the paper. Trace around it, then lift it up.
      2. Give your thumb an eye and a little triangle beak. Draw two feet below your palm.
      3. Your four fingers are the tail feathers. Make it your own! Try stripes, dots or a tiny hat.

      A Thanksgiving leaf-rubbing card

      Pick someone you’re thankful for. Find a leaf with bumpy veins, a sheet of paper, and a crayon with its wrapper peeled off.

      1. Lay the leaf bumpy side up under your paper. You can leave the sheet flat or fold it into a card.
      2. Rub the side of your crayon gently over the paper until the leaf appears. Take the leaf out and write “Happy Thanksgiving!” above your rubbing.
      3. Below the leaf or inside your card, finish “I’m thankful for you because…” with one little thing that person does. Draw the two of you together, sign your name, and give them your card.

      Rudolph’s shared wish jar

      Make one jar together for your family and everyone who visits. Gather a clean jar, brown paper, a red pom-pom, two paper eyes, glue, tape, a pencil and little slips of paper.

      1. Wrap brown paper around the jar. Tape it in place, then glue on two eyes and a red pom-pom nose. Keep the inside empty for folded wishes.
      2. Set the jar by the door with the slips and a sign: “Who are you thinking of? Write or draw one hope for them in 2027.” Fold each slip and write the person’s name on the outside.
      3. On Christmas Eve, take turns handing each note to the person it is for. Save notes for anyone who is away.

      Make a set. A second jar dressed as Santa can hold wrapped candy. Give a snowman jar a little sparkle with glitter glue on the outside.

      Try it at school. Decorate one class jar together. Your teacher can pair everyone up so each classmate gets a note. Write or draw a hope that starts with “In 2027, I hope you…” and put it in the jar. Hand out the notes before winter break.

      Green glitter-tree ornaments

      Gather green glitter cardstock, red dot stickers, scrap paper, a pencil, kid scissors, string, and tape.

      1. Fold the scrap paper in half. Starting at the fold, draw half a pine tree with three pointy branches and a short trunk. Cut along your pencil line and unfold your tree template.
      2. Trace the tree on the plain back of your glitter cardstock and cut it out. Decorate the branches with little red dots.
      3. Write your name and the year on the back. Tape both ends of a short string behind the treetop to make a hanging loop.

      Make a wooden-spoon reindeer door hanger

      Gather five wooden spoons, craft glue, twine, brown and cream felt, two googly eyes, a red pom-pom and a plaid ribbon.

      1. Lay one spoon bowl-down for the face. Fan four spoons behind it with their bowls pointing up for antlers. Ask a grown-up to help glue the handles together and wrap the join with twine.
      2. Cut two brown felt ears and two smaller cream centers. Glue them beside the face, then add the eyes and pom-pom nose.
      3. Tie a plaid bow below the nose and add a bell if you like. Fasten a twine loop at the back. Let the glue dry before hanging Rudolph.

      Make a sparkly snowflake tree ornament

      Gather four jumbo craft sticks, markers, craft glue, gems, a short piece of string, and tape.

      1. Color the sticks. Make a plus sign with two sticks. Cross the other two diagonally over the same center to make eight points.
      2. Glue the sticks where they cross. Lay the snowflake flat and decorate each arm with gems. Let the glue dry.
      3. Tie the ends of the string together. Tape the knot to the back of one arm and hang your snowflake on the tree.

      New Year wishing stars

      Gather paper, a pencil, crayons, kid scissors, string, and tape.

      1. Draw three big stars and cut them out.
      2. On each star, draw one thing you hope to try in the new year.
      3. Tape the stars along a piece of string and hang your wishes across a shelf.

      Snowflake window art

      Gather a square of thin paper and kid scissors.

      1. Fold the square corner to corner twice to make a small triangle.
      2. Snip little notches along the edges. Leave part of each folded edge connected.
      3. Open your snowflake slowly and hold it up to the light.

      Cozy paper cocoa

      Gather colored paper, glue, crayons, and white scrap paper.

      1. Draw a big mug with a handle. Color a brown oval at the top for cocoa.
      2. Tear tiny bits of white paper and glue them onto the cocoa for marshmallows.
      3. Draw curly steam lines and give your mug a pattern.

      Penguin finger puppet

      Gather black and white paper, orange crayon, tape, and kid scissors.

      1. Wrap a strip of paper loosely around two fingers. Slide it off and tape it into a tube.
      2. Cut out a black oval and glue or tape a smaller white paper belly onto it.
      3. Draw eyes and an orange beak. Tape the penguin to the tube and make it waddle.

      Winter birch forest

      Gather dark paper, white paper, a dark crayon, and glue.

      1. Tear long skinny strips of white paper for birch trunks.
      2. Glue the trunks to the dark sheet and add short dark marks across each trunk.
      3. Tear tiny white dots for falling snow. Hide a little drawn animal between the trees.

      Design a pair of skates

      Gather paper, crayons, foil, and tape.

      1. Draw two boot shapes with their toes pointing the same way.
      2. Decorate the boots with matching laces and different patterns.
      3. Fold two strips of foil into skinny blades and tape one under each boot.

      Pocketful of paper hearts

      Gather paper, crayons, kid scissors, and tape.

      1. Fold the bottom third of a sheet up and tape the sides to make a pocket.
      2. Cut little paper hearts that fit inside. Write or draw something kind on each.
      3. Decorate the pocket and give it to someone to open.

      A winter bird collage

      Gather scrap paper, glue, and crayons.

      1. Tear a round belly, a smaller head, and a wing from colored paper.
      2. Glue them together on a sheet and add a triangle beak and two stick legs.
      3. Draw a snowy branch under the bird and invent a name for it.

      Build a paper quilt

      Gather colored scrap paper, kid scissors, glue, and a sheet for the backing.

      1. Cut nine roughly equal paper squares.
      2. Draw a repeating pattern on each square. Try dots, zigzags, and tiny hearts.
      3. Arrange them in three rows and glue them down to make a quilt for a paper doll.

      Make a windy-day ribbon wand

      Gather a cardboard tube, strips of colored paper or ribbon, crayons, and tape.

      1. Decorate the tube with swirls and clouds.
      2. Tape several long streamers inside one end so they trail outside.
      3. Wave the tube slowly, then quickly. Which movement makes the biggest rainbow?
